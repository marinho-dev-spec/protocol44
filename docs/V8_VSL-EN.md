## Abertura principal

Someone asks for one more thing.

You say, “Of program.”

Then you look at your day and realise the time you had set aside for yourself is gone.

The decision you wanted to make. The conversation you wanted to prepare. The plan you were finally going to give some attention.

It moves to tomorrow.

If that feels familiar, bring one of those moments to mind. We’re going to work through a simple written exercise using something that actually happened in your week.

By the end, you’ll have a way to put the situation on paper and choose a next step you can try.

## Conexão e problema

You may already have thought carefully about what you want.

Perhaps you’ve journaled about it, listened to advice, or made a plan that looked good on Sunday.

Then the ordinary moments happen. A request arrives. Someone has an opinion. You miss the time you set aside, and the thought comes up: “I never stick with anything.”

Knowing what you want can leave you with another question:

“What do I do when this happens again?”

That’s the question this program works with. The quiz uses “Blocked Signal” as a metaphor for the interruption between an intention and a response. It does not mean your thoughts are a physical signal.

It is a guided personal development program about recurring patterns, the expectations that shape your choices, and practicing a response you want to use in real life.

Your responsibilities matter. Your time and resources matter. We include them in the exercise, so the next step fits the situation you’re actually in.

## Demonstração

Let’s use a fictional example from the first video session.

Maya planned to review her spending on Sunday. A family visit ran late, and she missed the time she had set aside.

On Monday morning, she thought, “I never stick with this.”

Then she put the notebook away.

There are three things to look at here.

First, what happened: she missed a planned review.

Second, what she told herself: she never follows through.

Third, what she could do next: open the notebook on Monday and check one upcoming bill. She could schedule the rest of the review separately.

That next step is specific enough to try. It also leaves room to see what happens and adjust.

Now use a moment from your own week.

Write one sentence about what happened. Keep it as close to the event as you remember it.

Then write what you told yourself.

Finally, choose one thing you could do next and when you could try it.

If your moment was an automatic yes, the next step might be preparing a sentence for the next request:

“Let me check what I can take on and get back to you.”

If it was a decision you kept postponing, your step might be writing down the information you still need.

If you dropped a plan, you might choose one part to return to.

You can pause here and use the practice page below. Leave a question open if you don’t know the answer yet.

## O que a prática abre

Once you have a situation on paper, you have something concrete to work with.

You can ask whether a familiar rule still fits. Whether you agreed because you wanted to, or because you felt you should. What you would like to say next time. What would help you return if the plan changes.

The program develops that work through three phases.

The Reading helps you look at the situation, the interpretation and the expectations shaping your response.

The Move helps you prepare and practice a choice: a boundary, a request, or an action that reflects what matters to you.

The Hold helps you apply it, review it and prepare a way to return after an interruption.

You can carry one situation through the program or choose another when it becomes more useful.
